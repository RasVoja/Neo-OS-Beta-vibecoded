Architecture

How Neo is put together

One master of the system, dedicated chips around it and one shared memory. Nothing works as a separate machine fighting for control.

Processor

ARM leads, the FPGA changes shape

Main CPU
A fast ARM runs Neo OS and translates x86 programs (FEX/Box64 under Bridge).
FPGA + CGRA
Chameleon loads retro cores (Z80, 6502, 68030) and accelerators.
Satellite x86
A separate x86 coprocessor for Windows, DOS and drivers, like the Bridgeboard card of old.
Chipset

Modern custom chips

Graphics
VSA-6000 (3D chiplets), Ray (ray tracing and blitter), Panorama (up to six displays, layers, AV1 encoder).
Others
Newton (physics), Orpheus (sound), Copernicus (Copper 2), Neuron (AI), Courier (I/O), Chronos (state snapshot).
The future

One Neo package

Chiplets
ARM, FPGA, VSA-6000 and an optional x86 in one package, connected over UCIe, with HBM memory.
Memory
Coherent (CXL), so all parts see the same data without copying.

Ports

Back panel

The same layout on Neo 2000 and 4000. Analogue VGA goes through a quality RAMDAC output from Panorama, in the spirit of the best graphics cards of the late nineties.

Software

Neo OS at work

The Neo Platinum look. On the desktop are the logical volumes (System:, Home:, RAM:, Boxes:) and an inserted cartridge. Chameleon holds the 68030 core in the FPGA, Satellite shows Windows as a Panorama layer, and the error message carries a code and a suggested fix.

Dijana

A living resident, not a pushy assistant

Where she lives
She sleeps in the corner of the menu bar. When she has a useful tip, she just raises her ears. Click if you want, otherwise she keeps sleeping.
When she speaks up
At first power-on, after an error (to explain what happened) and when you call her by voice or with a key. Never in the middle of typing.
Learns
Every tip only once. “I don't need this” turns off that whole kind of tip, and after being ignored three times she goes quiet on her own. She never sells anything.
Sniffing

“Dijana, sniff out…”

Search
A basset is a scent hound, so search sniffs: Dijana lowers her nose and follows the trail, and the results come up as you type.
Lost
For a lost file she says where and when it was last seen: “It was in the Mailbox yesterday at 18:20, then you moved it to Work.”
Humour

Trying to eat a document

When
Rarely: when you drag a document to the Bin, she sometimes catches it in her teeth and asks “May I?”, then drops it and wags her tail.
Limits
She never really touches the document, never in the middle of work and never more than once a day. Jokes are turned off with one switch.
On the desk

A small presence that doesn't get in the way

Behaviour
Sometimes she walks along the shelf, lies down next to a program that runs for a long time and wags her tail when Guardian has finished a backup. When the Sun battery runs low, she lies down in the shade.
Peace
While you type or watch a film, she doesn't move. On battery she isn't animated, so as not to use power.

Beauty without the burden

Glass, tiles and physics, while the processor rests

The best from the era when computers loved to show off: tiles with live data, transparent glass, a 3D window overview and physics like in games. The difference is that on Neo all of this is done by dedicated chips (Ray for 2D and glass, Newton for physics, VSA-6000 for 3D, Panorama for screen layers), so the main processor and the battery barely feel it. Nothing slows down work, and everything turns off with one switch.

Tiles

Live data on the desk, the shelf or in the Nook

What they are
Small live cards: weather, calendar, Turntable, exchange rate, Sun station, Guardian backups, a note, a clock for another city. The idea of the Vista-era sidebar, but without the slowness.
How they work
Each tile is a separate layer in Panorama and refreshes only when its data changes, at most once a second. When you can't see it, it does nothing.
Safety
A tile gets only the data it displays, through Keys. It cannot read files or send data without permission. They are made in Neo Kit in an afternoon.
Glass

Transparent, but always readable

Where
The menu bar, window titles, sidebars, Shelves and tiles are made of frosted glass through which the background shows. Text and documents are always on a solid, opaque backing.
Contrast
Ray measures the background under the glass and increases the blur or darkening by itself, so text never drops below the required contrast. With the Contrast theme the glass becomes solid.
Light
Window shadows follow the time of day: short and cool in the morning, long and warm in the evening. The reflection on the glass shifts slightly when you move a window.
Newton

Physics that helps, not just entertains

Windows
A thrown window glides and slows down, then bounces off the edge of the screen or moves to another monitor. Windows spread apart slightly so they don't overlap when there are many.
Bin
A document crumples up and flies in an arc into the Bin. The Bin fills visibly, and emptying is a short shake.
Shelves
Icons on the shelves move aside when you drag something, and a program that is starting bounces gently. A folder dragged over another folder springs open.
Seasons
In winter snow falls and settles on window edges and the shelf, in autumn leaves, in summer plums from Dijana's backyard. Move a window and the snow falls off. Only at the Lavish level, and never over a document.

Rules

Beautiful, but not a burden

Levels
Calm: no motion and no glass, for work and weaker eyes. Lively (default): glass, tiles, short animations and window physics. Lavish: everything, plus seasons, the 3D overview and a live background.
Speed
No animation lasts longer than 150 ms and each one is interrupted by the next click. Work never waits for an effect.
Power
On battery and in Efficient mode the system lowers the level by one by itself. Estimate for Neo 2000: Lively takes under 3% of the GPU, Lavish under 10%, and the main processor almost nothing.
Accessibility
“Less motion” and “No transparency” apply across the whole system and in all apps from Neo Kit.
Orpheus

Sound as part of the experience

Good sound tells you what happened before you look: it arrived, it worked, it failed, it's done. The rule is the same as for beauty: useful, short and quiet, and every sound also has a caption.

Meaning

Every sound says something

Language
Success goes up, an error goes down and short, a notification is two tones, and a finished long job is a small chord. The same rule applies in every theme, so the meaning is learned once.

All sounds of one theme are in the same key, so even when two overlap they sound like a chord, not noise.

Sound themes

Themes in the Treasury, like Residents and alongside them

What a theme is
A pack of up to 40 sounds for system events, plus power-on and shutdown sounds and an optional ambience. Free with the system: Neo Basic, Crystal, Wood and Chip.
Buying
In the Treasury, once and forever, without a subscription. The author gets most of the revenue, so local composers and sound designers get a market. Community themes come free under a free licence.
With a resident
Every resident can bring its own theme, and they are also sold separately. The dancing snake resident from “Ide zmija” gets a theme with parts of the song for power-on and a finished job, but only with the official licence of DJ Nick DeeTronic.
Mixing
Each event can come from a different theme: power-on from Chip, the Bin from Wood. An app can have its own theme, for example Arcade.
Open format
A theme is Opus or FLAC files and one text list of events. It is made in Wave or the Orpheus synthesizer and shared as a single neopak file.
Checking
The Treasury checks duration and loudness (all themes equally loud) and that an error is clearly distinguishable from success. Themes may not contain ads or speech that sells something.

Modern Neo

Six principles and what they change

Each principle gets a part in the OS and a part in hardware. The hardware part is there so software can't bypass it.

Flow

It doesn't matter where a program runs

OS
The Dispatcher chooses where to run by itself: native ARM, x86 translation, Satellite, an FPGA core or another Neo in the house. The user only sees an icon and launches it.
Hardware
Coherent memory (CXL) between the ARM, the FPGA and Satellite, so a program moves without copying data.
Renewal

One update for everything

OS
One signed system image: the kernel, all drivers, Chameleon cores, Satellite firmware and VSA-6000 microcode. It is written to the other partition while you work, and going back is one button. Drivers as a separate item don't exist.
Hardware
Dual Neo ROM (A/B), so even an interrupted update doesn't leave the machine unable to boot. Service mode via USB-B.
Treasury

Open source

OS
Every app in the Treasury has a Source code button and a reproducible build: the system checks that the installed program matches the published code. Neo Kit and Lux under free licences.
Hardware
Open schematics for the cartridge, AGP-X and the Satellite connector. FPGA cores published as source code.
Fortress

A safe, fast and stable kernel

OS
A formally verified microkernel (seL4 class) at the bottom. Exec-N and the system services in Rust. Linux remains only as an isolated driver domain: when a driver crashes, the domain restarts, not the system.
Hardware
ARM memory tagging (MTE), an IOMMU for every device, encrypted RAM. Satellite and PCIe cards see only the memory assigned to them.
Lock + Safe

Lock against data leaks

OS
Every file carries a label: Personal, Work or Public. Labelled data doesn't leave the machine without confirmation. A per-app firewall shows who sent what.
Hardware
The Safe chip keeps the keys and encrypts the disks. Physical switches for the camera, microphone and network cut the power, and software cannot turn off the LED next to them.

OS
Panorama layers serve as scenes for streaming. Neuron does background removal, framing and subtitles in Serbian. Montage works on proxy footage, without waiting for a render.
Hardware
An Eye camera included with every model (S, M, Pro), with a mechanical shutter, HDMI input for capturing another machine, AV1 encoder in Panorama, Orpheus microphone preamp.

Compressing each EXE separately (like UPX) breaks memory sharing and signatures, so it is done at the file system level.

Like the Amiga

A RAD: disk that survives a restart

What it is
The RAD: volume (working disk) in RAM, as fast as memory. Unlike RAM:, it survives a warm restart, a program crash, an update through Renewal and a change of FPGA core.
Hardware
The memory controller keeps the RAD: zone in self-refresh while the rest of the system resets, and Neo ROM doesn't erase it at boot. The zone is encrypted with a key from the Safe chip, and every block has a checksum, so a damaged block is recognized.
Size
Grows and shrinks as needed, compressed via Courier. Proposed limit: 4 GB on Neo 500, 16 GB on Neo 2000 and 32 GB on Neo 4000.
Shutdown
A cold shutdown erases RAD:, unless “Save RAD: at shutdown” is on. Then Courier compresses it to the SSD in a couple of seconds and restores it at the next power-on. The Neo 500 on battery keeps it even when the power goes out.
For what
Temporary projects and compiling programs, floppy and disk images for retro cores (Chameleon can boot directly from RAD:), the Horizon and Arcade cache and resuming work after a restart.

A long press on the Chronos button at power-on or the “Empty RAD:” command. Lock labels apply here too.
Standard format

Open zstd for almost everything and LZ4 when speed matters most. Courier does them in hardware in both directions, compression and decompression. We don't make our own algorithm, so that Neo files can be opened on any other computer.

File system, ROM image, neopak apps, Chronos snapshots, RAM (zram), network transfer, backups and cartridges.

Future
Every compressed block carries an algorithm and version tag. A new algorithm is added through Renewal, and old files stay readable forever.
Swarm

Processor, threads and multitasking: everything is a series of small jobs

Under the hood Neo splits everything into small jobs that last a fraction of a millisecond. One job queue fills all cores and all chips at once: a short job stays on the processor and runs with SIMD instructions (NEON and SVE2, the modern successors of MMX), and a long data stream goes to the chip made for it. It is the spirit of BeOS, where everything was threaded, but with today's job scheduling instead of manual thread management.

One frame of the game “Storm over Zlatibor” (16.6 ms at 60 frames per second): who does what

Swarm

Many small jobs instead of a few big threads

How
A program doesn't create threads by hand. It tells Neo Kit “this can be done in parallel”, and Swarm cuts it into small jobs and distributes them to free cores. A core that finishes takes a job from its neighbour.
The same queue for the chips
A job can carry a tag like “best on Newton” or “best on Neuron”. Swarm sends it there if the chip is free and the job is big enough, otherwise the processor does it.
For developers
The same code runs on the Neo 500 with few cores and on the Neo 4000 with many, just faster. There is no manual data transfer, because everything shares one memory. Ported Linux programs (WebKit, LibreOffice, games via Bridge) keep their threads, and the Dispatcher sorts them into classes.
SIMD

MMX's successors for small jobs

What
NEON and SVE2 do the same operation on many numbers at once, and SME multiplies matrices. That is what MMX and SSE did on the PC.
When the processor, when a chip
Sending a job to a chip costs a few microseconds. A short job (a sound effect, a small picture, encryption, game maths) finishes on SIMD before it would even reach the chip. A long stream (a film, a scene, physics of a whole world) goes to the chip.
The system decides
Swarm measures and learns where each job is faster, so the developer doesn't have to choose.
Cores

Big and efficient, without hyper-threading

Proposal
4 to 8 big ARM cores and 4 efficient ones. Responsiveness depends on the strongest core, and throughput on the number of cores.
Hyper-threading
Hardware hyper-threading, where one core pretends to be two, isn't needed: it gives little and makes work less predictable. Software threads and jobs are the basis of everything.
Satellite
MMX, SSE and AVX remain important only on Satellite x86 and in translating old programs and games.
Lesson

Idea

The programmer had to move data into those small memories by hand, so development was hard and slow. The next console switched to an ordinary processor.
Neo
Our chips are like SPE units with a clear job, but they share one memory, and the hard parts are done by system libraries and Swarm.

Hardware

What the machine must have

Controller

Neo for everyone

Neo 100 and Neo Revive: the same spirit for much less money

A Neo 500 and a cheap OLED screen are still too much for much of the world. So the family gets two steps below: Neo 100, a single ARM board in a keyboard, and Neo Revive, the same system on old computers. Both exchange files and work in the same community as the big Neo.

The Neo family from €0 to €4,500

Neo 100

A computer in a keyboard for €60 to 90

What
One ARM chip of the single-board computer class (4 cores, 4 to 8 GB), eMMC, WiFi, two USB ports, HDMI and composite output. No FPGA, VSA-6000 or Satellite.
Screen
Any TV, a used monitor or a cheap 10" IPS panel (about €30). Nothing has to be bought with it.
Power
About 5 W over USB-C. Runs from a power bank or a small 20 W solar panel.
Neo Revive

An old computer, a new life

What
Neo OS Lite boots from a USB stick on old laptops, PCs and ARM tablets. Fortress (seL4) runs on both x86 and ARM.
Why
Used laptops cost €0 to 50, and would otherwise become e-waste.
Where
Schools, libraries and clubs with an installation stick and a list of tested computers.
Doesn't run everywhere
Only on tested computers: 64-bit Intel or AMD with UEFI, roughly from 2012, at least 2 GB, Intel or AMD graphics (Nvidia only basic), and a few ARM boards of the single-board computer class. An old 32-bit PC can't. Before installation the stick checks the computer and says whether it will work.
Drivers
The only exception to the rule about our own drivers: for old PCs Linux driver code is used, locked in isolated parts of Fortress.

Easy mode is the default, and apps are chosen for 1 to 2 GB of RAM.

Path to a standard

Neo as a national computer

A country that brings Neo into its schools and administration gets a free OS, a licence for local assembly and manufacturing, and a translation into its own language. Socialist and social democratic countries are the natural first partners, and the door stays open to all. For countries under sanctions a legal opinion is needed.

Path to a standard · hardware

From prototype to open standard

The goal is for Neo to become what the IBM PC and MSX were: a specification by which several factories build compatible computers. We don't reinvent what already exists. Wherever there is a world standard, Neo uses it, and makes its own standard only for what is truly new. Timelines are estimates.

We adopt

World standards, unchanged

Links
PCIe, CXL, UCIe for chiplets, USB-C with power delivery, M.2 and NVMe, DisplayPort and HDMI, COM Express as the basis for the Satellite module.
Software
Vulkan, UEFI and ACPI following the ARM SystemReady rules, so another OS can be booted on Neo too. zstd, LZ4 and EROFS for compression.
We make

Only what is new

Connectors
The Neo cartridge (mechanics, NVMe and signature), AGP-X (PCIe ×16 + CXL + a channel for Panorama layers), the Satellite connector, the DE-9 joystick pinout.
Formats
Custom chip registers, the signed Chameleon core format, neopak, the Chronos snapshot and the Neo ROM layout.
Two levels

Neo Base and Neo Full

Basis
ARM core, FPGA fabric with Chameleon, Copernicus, Panorama, Orpheus, Courier, Safe, Neo ROM, cartridge slot, privacy switches. Enough for Neo OS, Arcade and school.
Full
Adds VSA-6000, Ray, Newton, Neuron and Chronos. Programs ask the system what is present, so they run on Base too, only slower.

Logo
Whoever passes the tests and pays for the certificate may use the name “Neo”. Whoever doesn't pay may build a compatible computer under their own name.

Independence

No single point of failure

Processor
ARM requires a licence, so the Neo standard is written above the instruction level. A RISC-V core is the licence-free fallback, and Neo Kit programs are simply recompiled.
Foundries
Chiplets on proven processes offered by several foundries, so changing foundry isn't the end of the project.
