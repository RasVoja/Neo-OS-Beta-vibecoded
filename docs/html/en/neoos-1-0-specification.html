1. What NeoOS 1.0 is

NeoOS 1.0 is the first version shipped to customers on all three models (Neo 500, 2000 and 4000), running from the Neo ROM, with every in-box app marked P0 and P1 and the launch games. Version 1.0 is a promise that the system can be the only computer in the house: mail, web, office, photos, music, learning, games and retro, with no account and no telemetry.

2. Layered architecture

NeoOS has seven layers. The bottom three are the guards: they decide who may do what. The top four are the workers: everything the user sees is there. The rule is simple: a layer above can never bypass the layer below.

Language and speed

The system is written in Rust, and hand-written ARM assembly (SVE2) is used only for the hottest 1 to 2% of code: memory copies, cryptography, codecs and blitter routines, measured before and after. An OS written entirely in assembly would not be noticeably faster, because the speed comes from the design (no copying, everything in RAM, accelerators, priority for input and sound), and it would be several times slower to build, less secure and tied to one generation of ARM.

Four design rules

  1. Responsiveness before throughput. Input, sound and window drawing have a guaranteed share of CPU time in Fortress. Nothing in the background may stall the mouse.
  2. One system, one API, one look. Neo Kit is the only windowing toolkit; foreign programs (Linux, Windows) get the same frames through the compositor and Link.
  3. Every app is scriptable. A Lux port in every program, like ARexx on the Amiga.
  4. Small and understandable. Every system app has a memory and size budget, after SymbOS. Going over budget is a build error.

3. Kernel: Fortress

Fortress is an seL4-class microkernel: small (about 10,000 lines), formally verified code that does only three things. It shares out memory, schedules processor time and passes messages between the protected parts of the system. Above it sit native drivers for all Neo hardware, written in Rust as separate processes, and the system services. Linux remains in only a few confined places, with no access to built-in hardware. When anything above the kernel crashes, Fortress restarts it and the rest of the system carries on.

Why programs still use the Linux ABI: WebKit, GStreamer, LibreOffice and thousands of libraries expect POSIX and Linux system calls, and writing our own POSIX personality from scratch would add a year. A game over Vulkan loses less than 3% per frame on that path, which is part of the month-12 gate.

Exec-N across domain boundaries

Messages between programs and services travel through shared-memory rings that Gate approves, without copying (similar to virtio queues). A port has a name (Cabinet.1, Gramophone), so a Lux script or a voice command does not need to know which domain the program lives in.

Fallback plan

The gate after month 12 checks whether Fortress meets its targets: IPC overhead under 5% on the Cabinet benchmark, graphics overhead under 3% per frame, and a correct restart of every driver and domain. If it does not, Genode moves to a different kernel (NOVA or base-hw), and the last resort is pKVM (protected KVM from Android). Either way, native drivers stay native and only the layer beneath them changes. We lose the formal proof of the kernel but keep everything else, and the plan slips by about 2 months instead of 6 or more.

6b. Neo Sound: Hi-Fi speakers, headphones, and libraries of sound and knowledge

Good sound does not have to be loud or expensive. The idea is the same as with graphics: cheap, honest hardware, with the quality drawn out by software. Orpheus already has a DSP, room correction and HRTF. Here it gets its own speakers and headphones and knows exactly how they sound, so it can correct them better than expensive parts would on their own.

Principles

  • Low power, high quality: the speakers are for a desk, a room and a classroom, not a party. The goal is clean, flat sound at normal volume, not decibels.
  • Wood and repair: wooden cabinets, parts held with screws, standard drivers that can be bought separately, and spare parts and a repair guide for at least 10 years.
  • Measured, not promised: every set has its measured response published, before and after Orpheus correction.
  • Healthy hearing: the system keeps track of how much sound the user has listened to and protects children's hearing.

The sound path through Orpheus

What expensive speakers do with analogue parts, Neo does in software, on the Orpheus DSP, with no load on the processor:

  1. Room correction: once at set-up, with the microphone in the Eye camera or the headphones, from three listening positions.
  2. Speaker profile: every model is measured at the factory, and Orpheus corrects the frequency and phase response with an FIR filter. Autoconfig recognises Neo Sound over USB and loads the profile by itself.
  3. Crossover: in software, between 80 and 120 Hz, with phase and delay adjusted to where the woofer stands.
  4. Loudness: at low volume the bass and treble are lifted gently along equal-loudness curves, as on old amplifiers.
  5. Protection: limits cone travel and coil temperature, so a small driver cannot be damaged even at full volume.

RAD: the RAM disk that survives a restart

On the Amiga, the RAD: disk stayed in place after a warm reset (Ctrl + Amiga + Amiga), so programs and files loaded instantly. NeoOS brings it back under the same name, RAD:, which is a generic abbreviation and not a protected name.

  • How it survives: on a warm restart the boot loader in NOR does not clear the reserved part of memory, and Fortress mounts it again. Every block has a checksum; a damaged disk is not mounted and the user gets a message explaining why.
  • What it survives: a warm restart, a change of FPGA core in Chameleon, a restart after a Renewal update and a restart after a system crash. It does not survive a power cut, except on the Neo 500 while the battery lasts.
  • Size: at most 4 GB on the Neo 500, 16 GB on the Neo 2000 and 32 GB on the Neo 4000; it only takes as much memory as its files need.
  • Security: the contents are encrypted like the rest of RAM, and Safe keeps the key only across a warm restart. Lock labels apply here too.
  • Booting from it: as on the Amiga, the system can start from RAD:, which developers use to try out test images and FPGA cores quickly.
  • At shutdown: the option "Keep RAD: at shutdown" writes the contents to the SSD through Courier (compressed, in a couple of seconds) and restores them at the next power-on.

Memory without swap

The rule: NeoOS never writes memory to disk page by page. Everything that matters for speed stays in RAM, and whatever must survive a restart lives in RAD:. There is enough memory for this: 32 GB on the Neo 500, 64 GB on the Neo 2000 and 96 GB on the Neo 4000.

The program in the foreground is never touched.
  • Power cut: on the Neo 2000 and 4000 an on-board ultracapacitor gives a few seconds of runtime, enough for Courier to write a compressed copy of RAD: to the SSD; on the Neo 500 the battery does this. Everything comes back at the next power-on. ECC memory and periodic scrubbing protect the contents while the computer runs.
  • How much faster it is: RAM is about 100 times faster than an NVMe drive, but the system already keeps recently used files in cache, so the gain is not a hundredfold. Estimate: the first launch of a program after power-on goes from about 300 to under 100 ms, opening a document is practically instant, and getting back to work after a warm restart drops from about 10 s to about 2 s. In work that is already "warm" the difference is small, about 10 to 20%. It is felt most after power-on and restarts, and on the Neo 500 with its slower drive.

Security in 1.0

  • Secure boot: the NOR boot loader checks the image's signature with the Safe key, and only then starts Fortress. An unsigned image does not boot, and service mode over USB-B restores the factory image.
  • ARM MTE for Rust and C++ services, an IOMMU for every device, encrypted RAM; Satellite and PCIe cards see only the memory windows assigned to them over CXL.
  • Physical switches for the camera, microphone and network, with an LED that software cannot turn off.
  • Treasury: reproducible builds, and the system checks that an installed program matches its published source code.
  • Error messages with a code (for example NEO-FS-017) that say what happened, why, and what the user can do.

7d. Crunching: programs and data on the fly

On the Amiga, programs such as Turbo Imploder and PowerPacker "crunched" executables: a program took half the space on a floppy and unpacked itself as it loaded. NeoOS brings the idea back, but as a service of the system rather than a trick inside the program. It applies to the full NeoOS and to Neo OS Lite alike.

Does it still make sense

WhereWhat you gain
Neo 500, 2000, 4000 (NVMe, Neo ROM)mostly space: programs and documents take about 30 to 50% less. Loading changes little because NVMe is already fast, but nothing is lost: Courier decompresses in hardware (part 3), with no processor work.
Neo 100 and Neo Revive (SD card, eMMC, USB stick, old SATA disk)space and speed: when the disk is slower than decompression, fewer bytes read means a faster start. On an old disk or a USB 2.0 stick a program can load up to twice as fast.
Treasury and Renewalsmaller downloads, which matters most on a slow or expensive connection and for country packs.

The figures are estimates from published measurements of zstd and LZ4 on typical executables and documents; the lab measures the real values on all three models and on the Revive computer list.

Crunched programs

  • No unpacker inside the program. Imploder and UPX put a small unpacker at the start of a program, so the program rewrites itself when it starts; antivirus tools often flag that falsely, and the memory cannot be shared. On Neo a program is an ordinary ELF file and the system loader (part 4, Exec-N) decompresses it on the fly.
  • In 64 KB chunks, like the EROFS image: only the parts of a program that are actually used are loaded, and a clean code page can be dropped from memory and read again from disk as if it were not crunched.
  • The signature stays valid. The program's content is what gets signed, and each chunk has its own checksum, so Safe verifies a program whether it is crunched or not.
  • When: Treasury ships neopak packages already crunched (part 7). Programs from other sources are crunched at installation or later, in the background, while the computer is on mains power and idle.
  • Dictionaries for small programs: tiles, Helpers and Neo DOS commands are small, so they are crunched with a shared zstd dictionary built from system code. Even a 40 KB program gets a decent saving that way.
  • Linux programs in the Applications domain are left as they are: the file system underneath crunches them.

Data crunched on the fly

For documents and data, compression is built into the file system, so no program sees it: programs read and write ordinary files, and on disk they are stored crunched. The Storage domain uses Btrfs (part 3), which can already do this.

PlaceAlgorithmWhy
System: (ROM)EROFS with LZ4 and zstdalready so in part 7; Courier decompresses

  • A level per file and folder: in the Info window ("Crunch: no, fast, strong, strongest") and with the Neo DOS command Crunch Home:Archive LEVEL 19 ALL; Decrunch undoes it.
  • No wasted time: files that are already compressed (JPEG, video, MP3, ZIP) are recognised by their first bytes and left as they are.
  • A copy to a USB stick or a mail attachment always goes out as an ordinary file, readable on any computer.

Why not RAR

RAR is a closed format: its author lets other programs only extract it, and packing into RAR is reserved to the author's own software. So RAR cannot be the packing format of an open system. Little is lost: xz and zstd at their highest levels save space in the same class as RAR5, and they are open and quick to unpack.

  • RAR archives open through libarchive (open source, RAR 4 and most of RAR5), in a sandbox like every decoder (part 4, Datatypes). The official unrar is an add-on in Treasury for the rare archives libarchive cannot read.
  • A recovery record, RAR's best feature (repairing a damaged archive), comes from open PAR2 data stored next to the archive: Archive and Keeper add it by themselves, for M-DISC and USB sticks.
  • Packing into ZIP and 7z (7-Zip, LGPL) for exchange with other computers.

The Disk tile and the "Crunch" button

  • The Disk tile (part 8i): the small face shows free space, the large face also shows what crunching has saved ("Saved 38 GB").
  • The "Crunch" button, a nod to Imploder: it re-crunches larger folders at a stronger level, in the background, while the computer is on mains power (Dispatcher, class 3). Progress in the Retro style shows calm colour bars in the spirit of Imploder's stripes, with no flashing.
  • In the Lite version the same button and tile; the default level is lower, and stronger crunching runs only on mains power.

Estimate: crunched programs in the loader, dictionaries, the compression policy in Storage, the Crunch and Decrunch commands, PAR2 with archives, the Disk tile: 15 person-months. Lite uses the same code.

How they are built

  • Isolated on Fortress. Every tile is a separate process with its own address space and a memory budget (target: up to 8 MB), like every system program. If one hangs, Fortress restarts it and a grey frame holds its place in the meantime; the other tiles, the Bar and the Desk carry on.
  • Only what it needs. A tile asks for capabilities in its manifest (Meter: read-only statistics; Weather: network access to one service). Gate grants only that, and Lock asks before the first trip onto the network. The lesson from Vista and Windows 7: their gadgets were retired in 2012 because they had too much access to the system; here a tile cannot do more than an ordinary program, only less.
  • They cost almost nothing. Live tiles are Panorama layers (part 8d) and refresh at most once a second; when hidden or when the screen is off they do not refresh at all. The Dispatcher keeps them in class 3 (background).
  • One frame in Neo Kit (NeoKit.Tile): a program describes three faces and Neo Kit draws the frame in the Retro, Future Shock or Crystal style. The compositor gets a new extension, neo-tile, so Linux programs can offer a tile too.
  • Scriptable. Every tile has a Lux port ("ADDRESS Clock / ALARM 7:00"), so voice, Ask Neo and the Neo MCP bridge see it like any other program.
  • Anyone can make one. A tile can be written in Lux or Neo BASIC in about ten lines; Neo School has a lesson called "Make your own clock". Tiles from Treasury are signed, like every package.

8j. Neo DOS: the Shell on an equal footing with the Desk

On the Amiga the desktop and the command line were two equal faces of the same system: LoadWB started the desktop, NewCLI opened a command window and EndCLI closed it. NeoOS brings that principle back. Because NeoOS is not Linux (Linux lives only in confined domains, part 3), our own shell has nothing to "mask": Neo DOS is the system's native shell, in the spirit of AmigaDOS and TRIPOS, and a Linux shell is an extra for those who want it.

Two faces, always at hand

  • The Shell is the Neo DOS window. It opens with Neo+Enter, from the Neo menu ("New Shell"), or by typing > in the command palette, which runs the command without opening a window.
  • A DOS screen under the Desk: as with drag-down screens (part 8), pull the Desk down and a full DOS screen is underneath. Neo+Esc lowers it and brings it back.
  • LoadDesk and EndDesk (LoadWB is accepted too, out of habit) start and stop the Desk. When the Desk is stopped, open programs go to sleep through Chronos and come back when it starts again; command windows and services keep running.
  • Starting without the Desk is a choice at start-up (the D key) and a mode for servers, home servers and repairs. Neo OS Lite ships with the Neo Desk, just like the full NeoOS; DOS alone is an option, never the default.

  • Linux programs from Treasury and the Linux box run in it as on any Linux.

What every offer guarantees, and what it does not offer

  • All the code is public and reproducibly built; a country can check every byte of the image in ROM.
  • The same privacy in every country: no hidden tracking, no keys to users' data, no exceptions for any authority. The national layer in Renewal carries software, certificates and translation; it cannot read or send users' files.
  • News stays the user's choice: a country pack may offer local sources, but none is switched on in advance.

How an adaptation runs

  1. Enquiry and assessment (2 weeks): language, script, schools, number of devices, and what already exists (translations in Weblate, voice models, Kiwix in that language).
  2. Contract with a clear scope and deadlines.
  3. Joint team: local translators and teachers work in a partner workspace (Weblate, with text reviewed in the context of the screen), and the Neo team reviews and merges.
  4. Pilot in 5 to 10 schools, measured as in section 1 (10 minutes without a manual) in that language.
  5. Signing and delivery of the country pack (below), then maintenance with every NeoOS release.

Team support as part of the OS

Neo Support is a system app: bug reports with logs only with consent (the user sees exactly what is sent), the status of a report, messages from the team, and expert remote help through "Call your own" (view only until the user says "yes", switches off after 30 minutes). For adaptation partners it also shows translation progress per part of the system and open questions. It works offline too: a report waits on the Worktable and is sent when a connection appears or through the Neighbourhood.
