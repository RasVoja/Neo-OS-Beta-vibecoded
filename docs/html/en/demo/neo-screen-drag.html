Screen drag: a homage to the Amiga

Grab the title bar of a full-screen program and pull it down: the desk shows underneath, or the program behind it. Let go and the screen stays where you dropped it, as on the Amiga. The second mode shows windows sliding into the Bar when they minimise, as in Windows. Simulated in the browser. planned for Lite

On the Amiga

Every program could open its own screen, with its own resolution and colours. Screens stood one behind another; you pulled a title bar down to see the screen behind, and the depth gadget changed the order. The video chip did it live, switching mode line by line.

In Neo Boing Platinum OS

  • No layered video modes any more: the graphics card sends one picture. A full-screen program is one surface in the compositor.
  • When you drag, the compositor only moves that surface and draws what is underneath: the desk or the next program. The program redraws nothing.
  • A program can run at its own resolution (for example 640 × 400 for old software); the compositor scales it, so the screen looks like its own.
  • A depth button, Super+M for the next screen, three fingers down on touch.
  • Windows slide into the Bar and back, as in Windows, with a choice of No motion / Calm / Brisk; it respects reduced motion.

When it comes

Planned for Lite, part of the compositor work (a buffer per window), after minimising to the Bar, which already works in the ISO. It needs no Neo hardware: it runs on an ordinary PC and on a Raspberry Pi 5. A video follows once the feature is in the code.
